How to Reduce Background Noise in Audio Recordings

Practical ways to remove hiss, hum and room noise from voice recordings on iPhone and Mac, plus recording habits that prevent noise in the first place.

By AppLayer Studio · Last reviewed October 24, 2026 · Checked on iOS 17 and later, macOS 14 and later

In this guide

  1. Step 1: Try the free built-in option
  2. Step 2: Use dedicated noise-reduction tools
  3. Don’t overdo it
  4. Prevent noise when recording
  5. Transcribing noisy audio

Short answer: for Voice Memos recordings, try Enhance Recording first. Open the recording, tap ⋯ → Edit Recording, then tap the magic-wand button. For more control, use an audio editor with dedicated noise reduction, apply it gently, then follow up with a noise gate and loudness normalization. No tool can fully rescue a very noisy recording, so the best results come from combining light cleanup with better recording habits.

Step 1: Try the free built-in option

Voice Memos → Enhance Recording reduces background noise and room echo with one tap:

  1. Open the recording in Voice Memos.
  2. Tap ⋯ → Edit Recording.
  3. Tap the Enhance (magic wand) button, listen, then tap Done.

It’s quick and often good enough for lectures and voice notes. You can’t adjust its strength, and it only works on Voice Memos recordings.

Step 2: Use dedicated noise-reduction tools

An audio editor gives you separate tools for different kinds of noise. Audio Edit for iPhone, iPad and Mac includes Noise Reduction, Noise Gate, Click Removal, Echo Removal and Voice Enhance. These are part of its Pro subscription, according to the App Store listing. A typical order:

  1. Trim first. Remove long pauses and the start and end of the recording, where noise is most obvious. See how to trim audio.
  2. Noise Reduction for constant noise, such as fan hiss, air conditioning or electrical hum. Start with a low setting and preview. Increase it only until the noise stops being distracting.
  3. Noise Gate for gaps. A gate silences quiet sections between phrases, which hides leftover hiss when nobody is talking.
  4. Click and Echo Removal if you hear mouth clicks or a boomy room.
  5. Voice Enhance or EQ to bring back clarity. A gentle low-cut filter removes rumble below the voice.
  6. Normalize or Loudness Normalization last, so the final file plays at a consistent volume.

Preview each effect before you apply it, and compare with the original. Audio Edit lets you hear effects live before exporting.

Don’t overdo it

Heavy noise reduction causes artifacts: voices start to sound “watery”, metallic or robotic, and word endings get swallowed. If that happens, back off the amount. A little natural room tone sounds better than distorted speech.

Noise reduction also can’t separate a voice from noise of the same kind, such as other people talking, loud music, or wind hitting the microphone. In those cases, re-recording is usually the only real fix.

Prevent noise when recording

The cleanest audio is recorded clean:

  • Get close to the mic. Halving the distance makes your voice much louder relative to the room. With an iPhone, hold it about 15–30 cm (6–12 in) from your mouth, slightly off to the side to avoid breath pops.
  • Choose a soft room. Curtains, carpets, sofas and closets full of clothes absorb echo. Bare kitchens and bathrooms add it.
  • Turn off noise sources. Fans, air conditioners, fridges and notifications. Use Do Not Disturb or Airplane Mode.
  • Record 5 seconds of silence at the start. It gives noise-reduction tools a clean sample of the background.
  • Use an external mic for regular podcasts or interviews. A wired lavalier is inexpensive and makes a big difference.

Transcribing noisy audio

If the goal is a transcript rather than polished audio, cleaner input still helps accuracy. Record close to the speaker, and consider an on-device transcription app such as AI Note Taker, which transcribes live while you record.
