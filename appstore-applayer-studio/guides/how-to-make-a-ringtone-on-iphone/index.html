How to Make a Custom Ringtone on iPhone

Turn any song clip, voice memo or video sound into an iPhone ringtone or text tone using GarageBand, a Mac, or a ringtone maker app.

By AppLayer Studio · Last reviewed October 24, 2026 · Checked on iOS 17 and later; menu names can vary by version

Short answer: iPhone ringtones must be added through Apple’s own channels. On the iPhone itself, that means exporting a clip of 30 seconds or less from Apple’s free GarageBand app. On a Mac, you can drag an .m4r file onto your iPhone in Finder. Ringtone maker apps such as ClipTone handle the trimming and hand the clip to GarageBand for you.

Below are all three methods, plus how to assign the tone once it’s on your phone.

Before you start

  • Use audio you have the right to use. Your own recordings, royalty-free tracks or music you’ve purchased as files work. Songs streamed from Apple Music, Spotify and similar services are DRM-protected and can’t be turned into ringtones.
  • Keep it short. Ringtones longer than 30 seconds are shortened by GarageBand. Text and alert tones work best at a few seconds.
  • Pick the moment. A ringtone loops, so start on a strong beat or a clear vocal line rather than a slow fade-in.

Method 1: GarageBand on iPhone (free, no computer)

GarageBand is made by Apple and free on the App Store. The trickiest part is getting your audio into a GarageBand project.

  1. Save the audio file to the Files app, for example in On My iPhone.
  2. Open GarageBand, create a new song, and choose the Audio Recorder instrument.
  3. Tap the Tracks view button (the icon of stacked bars) at the top.
  4. Tap the Loop Browser button (it looks like a loop of string), then the Files tab, then Browse items from the Files app. Pick your file.
  5. Drag the file from the list onto the track, aligning it to the start.
  6. Optional: drag the edges of the region to trim it, and turn off the metronome so it isn’t exported.
  7. Tap the down-arrow at the top left and choose My Songs to save the project.
  8. Long-press the project, tap Share, then Ringtone. Name it and tap Export.
  9. When the export finishes, tap Use sound as… and choose Standard Ringtone, Standard Text Tone, or Assign to contact.

If your clip is longer than 30 seconds, GarageBand will offer to shorten it.

Method 2: Use a ringtone maker app

GarageBand’s editor is designed for making music, so finding the exact second you want can be fiddly. A dedicated app makes the selection step easier.

With ClipTone: Ringtone Maker, you:

  1. Pick a video or audio file. ClipTone can pull the sound out of a video in your library.
  2. Drag the start and end handles on the waveform to select your moment, then preview it.
  3. Choose what it’s for: ringtone, alarm, text tone or charging sound.
  4. For ringtones and alert tones, ClipTone passes the clip to GarageBand. Then follow steps 8–9 above to export and assign it.

ClipTone also keeps a collection of everything you’ve made, so you can rename tones and switch between them later. It’s free to download, and full access requires a subscription with a free trial, as described on its App Store listing.

For more precise editing, such as fading the start and end, normalizing volume or cutting out a section, an audio editor like Audio Edit can export an .m4r ringtone file directly.

Method 3: From a Mac with Finder

If you already have an .m4r file, for example one exported from an audio editor:

  1. Connect your iPhone to your Mac with a cable and unlock it.
  2. Open Finder and select your iPhone in the sidebar.
  3. Drag the .m4r file onto the iPhone’s window in Finder.

The tone appears in the ringtone list on your iPhone. If nothing happens, make sure the file is under 40 seconds and has the .m4r extension, not .m4a.

Set the ringtone on your iPhone

Once a tone is on your phone:

  • Default ringtone: Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone, then pick your tone at the top of the list.
  • Text tone: Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Text Tone.
  • One contact: Contacts → choose a person → Edit → Ringtone or Text Tone.

Your custom tones appear above Apple’s built-in tones.

What about alarms and charging sounds?

  • Alarms: in the Clock app, edit an alarm and tap Sound. Custom ringtones you’ve added appear in the list.
  • Charging sounds: iOS doesn’t have a setting for this, but you can create one with a Shortcuts automation. In Shortcuts, open the Automation tab, create a new automation with the Charger → Is Connected trigger, set it to run immediately, and add a Play Sound action with your audio file. ClipTone includes charging-sound options that work with this approach.

Troubleshooting

  • “Ringtone” isn’t in GarageBand’s share menu: share the project from My Songs, not from inside the editor.
  • The tone is silent or cut off: check that the region starts at the very beginning of the timeline. Empty space before it is exported too.
  • Streaming songs won’t import: they’re DRM-protected. Use a file you own instead.
  • The tone disappeared after an update: custom tones are kept in your backups. Re-export from GarageBand or ClipTone’s collection if one goes missing.
