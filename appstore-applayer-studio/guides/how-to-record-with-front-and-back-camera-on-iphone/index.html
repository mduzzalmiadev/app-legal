How to Record with Front and Back Cameras at Once on iPhone

Record with both iPhone cameras at once for reactions and vlogs, check which iPhones support it, and get vertical and horizontal versions from one take.

By AppLayer Studio · Last reviewed October 24, 2026 · Checked on iOS 26; supported iPhone models vary by app

In this guide

  1. Check whether your iPhone supports it
  2. Record with DualShot
  3. Tips for dual-camera videos
  4. Reacting to video on your screen instead?

Short answer: recording with both cameras at once requires an iPhone and app that support simultaneous camera capture. Apple added this capability for developers on iPhone XS, XR and later, and some recent iPhones also include a dual-capture option in the Camera app. On other models, a dual-camera app such as DualShot records both cameras at once, with your face in a picture-in-picture bubble or side by side.

Check whether your iPhone supports it

  • Built-in Camera app: Apple added a dual-capture video mode to the Camera app on some recent iPhone models. Open Camera → Video and look for it in the controls. If you don’t see it, your model doesn’t support it.
  • Third-party apps: apps use Apple’s multi-camera feature, which needs a reasonably recent iPhone. Each app also has its own minimum iOS version. DualShot requires iOS 26 or later, according to its App Store listing. Check the listing’s Compatibility section for your device before downloading.

Record with DualShot

DualShot: Dual Camera Recorder is built around one idea: record once, get every format. According to its listing:

  1. Open DualShot and choose Dual Camera mode to use the front and back cameras together, or FaceCam for a movable selfie bubble.
  2. Pick one of the 11 cinematic filters if you want a look applied while recording.
  3. Optional: turn on the teleprompter and paste your script. It auto-scrolls while you record.
  4. Record. DualShot saves two synchronized videos: a vertical 9:16 version for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, and a horizontal 16:9 version for YouTube, plus matching photos.

Recording settings go up to 4K at 24/30/60 fps with HEVC compression, and export in MOV or MP4.

Tips for dual-camera videos

  • Stabilize the phone. With two cameras running, handheld shake is doubled. Use a tripod or rest the phone on something.
  • Light both sides. The front camera often faces you away from the window. Add a lamp or face the light source.
  • Mind the battery and heat. Two camera streams use more power. Keep recordings shorter or plug in for long sessions.
  • Free up space first. Two 4K files from every take fill storage quickly. See how to free up storage on iPhone.

Reacting to video on your screen instead?

If you want to react to a video or app on your phone rather than to the world around you, use screen recording with a face-cam overlay. See how to record a reaction video on iPhone, or Screen Recorder: Video Edits, whose Dual Camera tool also records front and back cameras in split or picture-in-picture layouts.
