How to Extract Audio from a Video on iPhone

Save just the sound from a video on your iPhone as an audio file, using a free Shortcut, an offline converter app, or an audio editor.

By AppLayer Studio · Last reviewed October 24, 2026 · Checked on iOS 17 and later; Shortcuts menu names can vary by version

In this guide

  1. Method 1: Build a free Shortcut (M4A output)
  2. Method 2: Use an offline converter app (MP3 output)
  3. Method 3: Extract and edit in one step
  4. Which format should I choose?
  5. Common problems

Short answer: iPhone has no “save audio only” button, but Apple’s free Shortcuts app can do it with the Encode Media action and its Audio Only option. If you’d rather tap a button, or you need MP3 instead of M4A, a converter app such as MP3 Audio Converter does the same thing on your device.

This works for videos you recorded or videos you have the rights to use, such as lectures, interviews, voice notes recorded as video, and your own music performances.

Method 1: Build a free Shortcut (M4A output)

You only need to set this up once.

  1. Open Shortcuts and tap + to create a new shortcut.
  2. Add the Select Photos action. Tap the arrow to expand it and set it to include Videos only.
  3. Add the Encode Media action. Expand it and turn on Audio Only.
  4. Add the Save File action so you can choose where the audio goes in the Files app.
  5. Rename the shortcut, for example “Video to Audio”, and tap Done.

Run it, pick a video, and choose a folder. You’ll get an .m4a file, which plays on any Apple device and in most apps.

Limitations: the output format is M4A (AAC). Some older car stereos, websites or tools only accept MP3, and Shortcuts can’t produce MP3 directly.

Method 2: Use an offline converter app (MP3 output)

MP3 Audio Converter is built for exactly this task:

  1. Open the app and choose a video from your Photos library.
  2. Tap Convert.
  3. Preview the result with the built-in player, then save or share the MP3.

According to its App Store listing, conversion runs entirely on your device, so videos aren’t uploaded anywhere and it works without an internet connection. It also keeps a history of past conversions. The same app has a native Mac version if you work with videos on your computer.

Method 3: Extract and edit in one step

If the audio needs work after you extract it, such as cutting out the start, removing background hum or boosting quiet speech, open the video directly in an audio editor. Audio Edit can pull the soundtrack out of a video and then lets you trim, apply noise reduction, normalize loudness and export to formats including MP3, M4A, WAV and FLAC.

And if what you really want is a ringtone, ClipTone extracts the sound from a video and lets you trim it straight into a tone. See how to make a custom ringtone.

Which format should I choose?

Format Best for Notes
M4A (AAC) Apple devices, smaller files Good quality at modest bitrates; the default for Shortcuts
MP3 Maximum compatibility Plays nearly everywhere
WAV Further editing Uncompressed, so files are large

For speech, a lower bitrate is usually fine. For music, choose a higher quality setting if your app offers one.

Common problems

  • The video is in iCloud Photos: it may need to download first. Make sure you’re online the first time you convert it.
  • No sound in the result: check that the original video actually has an audio track. Some screen recordings are made with the microphone off and system audio muted.
  • Videos from streaming apps: these aren’t in your Photos library and are usually protected, so they can’t be converted. Respect copyright and the platform’s terms.
