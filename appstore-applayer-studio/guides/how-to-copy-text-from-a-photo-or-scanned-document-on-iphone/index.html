How to Copy Text from a Photo or Scanned Document on iPhone

Use Live Text to copy text from photos and the camera on iPhone, and how OCR apps help with multi-page scans, searchable PDFs and pasting into forms.

By AppLayer Studio · Last reviewed October 24, 2026 · Checked on iOS 15 and later on iPhone XS, XR and newer

In this guide

  1. Copy text from a photo with Live Text
  2. Copy text straight from the camera
  3. Limitations of Live Text
  4. When an OCR scanner app is better
  5. Paste scanned text into forms faster
  6. Privacy tip

Short answer: open the photo in Photos and touch and hold any word. iPhone’s Live Text feature lets you select and copy text from images, and from the camera view in real time. For multi-page documents, searchable PDFs, or text you need to keep organized, use a scanner app with OCR (optical character recognition).

Copy text from a photo with Live Text

Live Text works on iPhone XS, iPhone XR and later models running iOS 15 or newer.

  1. Open the image in Photos. Screenshots work too.
  2. Touch and hold a word until it’s highlighted, then drag the handles to select more.
  3. Tap Copy. You can also choose Translate or Look Up.

Alternatively, tap the Live Text button (a small text-in-a-frame icon) at the bottom right of the photo to highlight all detected text, then tap Copy All.

Phone numbers, email addresses, dates and web links in the image become tappable, so you can call or open them directly.

Copy text straight from the camera

  1. Open Camera and point it at the text.
  2. When a yellow frame appears around the text, tap the Live Text button.
  3. Select the text you need and tap Copy.

This is handy for Wi-Fi passwords on a router label, serial numbers or a paragraph from a book.

Limitations of Live Text

  • It works best on printed text. Messy handwriting may not be recognized.
  • It works on one image at a time. For a 10-page document, you’d copy page by page.
  • Recognized text isn’t saved. If you open the image again, it’s recognized again, and the text doesn’t become searchable in Files.
  • Language support is limited to the languages Apple lists for Live Text in your region.

When an OCR scanner app is better

For paperwork you scan regularly, an app with built-in OCR keeps the text with the document. According to its App Store listing, PDF Scanner Pro can:

  • Scan single or multi-page documents with automatic edge detection.
  • Extract editable text from the scans, then copy, search or share it.
  • Recognize multiple languages.
  • Organize documents into folders with instant search.

This makes it easier to find a receipt or contract later by searching for words inside it.

Paste scanned text into forms faster

If you often copy text from photos into other apps, such as addresses from business cards or numbers from receipts, a keyboard with built-in OCR saves switching back and forth. TypeAI, a custom keyboard named AutoPaste inside the app, has an OCR page that recognizes text from a photo you pick and pastes it into the field you’re typing in. Its listing says this runs on-device using Apple’s Vision framework.

Privacy tip

Text from IDs, bank cards and medical letters is sensitive. Live Text runs on your device. If you use a third-party app, check its privacy policy and whether it processes images on-device or online.
