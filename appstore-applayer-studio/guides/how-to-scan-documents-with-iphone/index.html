How to Scan Documents with an iPhone

Scan paper documents to PDF with your iPhone camera using the Notes or Files app, plus tips for sharp, readable scans and when a scanner app helps.

By AppLayer Studio · Last reviewed October 24, 2026 · Checked on iOS 17 and later

In this guide

  1. Method 1: Scan straight to a PDF in Files
  2. Method 2: Scan into a note
  3. Tips for better scans
  4. When to use a scanner app
  5. Keep sensitive scans safe

Short answer: iPhone has a document scanner built into the Notes and Files apps. In Files, tap Browse, then the More (⋯) button, then Scan Documents. Point the camera at the page, and it captures, crops and straightens it automatically. Tap Save to get a PDF. Use a scanner app when you need OCR text, signatures, folders or a lot of scanning.

Method 1: Scan straight to a PDF in Files

  1. Open the Files app and tap Browse.
  2. Choose a location, such as On My iPhone or iCloud Drive.
  3. Tap the More button (⋯) at the top right and choose Scan Documents.
  4. Hold the iPhone above the page. In Auto mode it captures when the page is steady. In Manual mode, tap the shutter.
  5. Adjust the corners if the crop is off, then scan the next page or tap Save.

The result is a multi-page PDF in the folder you chose, ready to share or attach to email.

Method 2: Scan into a note

  1. Open Notes and create or open a note.
  2. Tap the camera button (or the paperclip, depending on your iOS version) and choose Scan Documents.
  3. Scan your pages and tap Save.

To get a PDF out of the note, tap the scan, then the Share button, and choose Save to Files or send it directly.

Tips for better scans

  • Contrast matters. Place light paper on a dark table, and dark paper on a light surface. The scanner finds edges more easily.
  • Even light, no shadows. Hold the phone so its shadow isn’t on the page, and avoid harsh overhead spotlights that cause glare on glossy paper.
  • Flatten the page. Press down curled receipts and book pages, since curves distort text.
  • Fill the frame, but leave a little background around all four edges so the corners can be detected.
  • Choose the right filter. Grayscale or black-and-white makes text crisp and files smaller. Keep color for photos, stamps or colored forms.

When to use a scanner app

The built-in scanner is great for occasional use. A dedicated app helps when you scan often or need more than a PDF. PDF Scanner Pro adds, according to its App Store listing:

  • OCR text recognition, to extract, copy and search the text in your scans.
  • Signatures and annotations, to sign and fill out forms without printing.
  • Folders, renaming and search, to keep receipts, contracts and notes organized.
  • Filters and adjustments for brightness, contrast, grayscale and black-and-white.
  • Export as PDF or images, or print directly from your phone.

Its listing also says scanning works offline. Unlimited scanning and OCR require a subscription.

If you already have photos of documents rather than fresh scans, see how to convert photos to PDF.

Keep sensitive scans safe

Scans of IDs, bank statements and contracts are sensitive. Before sharing, crop out details the recipient doesn’t need, store scans in a location protected by your passcode or Face ID, and delete copies from your camera roll if you photographed the documents instead of scanning them.
