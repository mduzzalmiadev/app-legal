How to Edit Audio on a Mac

Trim, convert and edit audio on a Mac with QuickTime Player, the Music app and GarageBand, and when a dedicated audio editor is worth it.

By AppLayer Studio · Last reviewed October 24, 2026 · Checked on macOS 14 and later

In this guide

  1. Quick trim with QuickTime Player
  2. Convert formats with the Music app
  3. Multi-track editing with GarageBand
  4. When a dedicated audio editor makes sense
  5. Choosing the right tool

Short answer: for a quick trim, open the file in QuickTime Player and choose Edit → Trim. To convert formats, the Music app can create MP3 or AAC versions. For multi-track editing, effects and noise cleanup, use GarageBand (free) or a dedicated editor such as Audio Edit, which has a native Mac version.

Quick trim with QuickTime Player

QuickTime Player comes with every Mac and trims audio as well as video.

  1. Right-click the audio file → Open With → QuickTime Player.
  2. Choose Edit → Trim (⌥⌘T).
  3. Drag the yellow handles to the part you want to keep. Hold an arrow key while dragging for finer control.
  4. Click Trim, then File → Export As (or Save) to write a new file.

It can’t fade, cut out the middle, or apply effects, but it’s the fastest option for removing dead air at the start and end.

Convert formats with the Music app

To make an MP3 or AAC copy of a file in your Music library:

  1. In Music, open Settings → Files → Import Settings and choose the encoder (MP3 or AAC) and quality.
  2. Select the song, then choose File → Convert → Create MP3 Version (or AAC).

The converted copy appears next to the original in your library.

Multi-track editing with GarageBand

GarageBand is free from Apple and handles multi-track projects. It’s ideal for podcasts with music beds or for combining several takes.

  • Drag audio files into the tracks area.
  • Split regions with Edit → Split Regions at Playhead (⌘T) and drag them to rearrange.
  • Use each track’s Smart Controls and plug-ins for EQ and compression.
  • Export with Share → Export Song to Disk.

GarageBand is designed around music, so tasks such as noise reduction, vocal removal or batch format conversion aren’t built in.

When a dedicated audio editor makes sense

Audio Edit: Audio Editor Pro runs natively on Mac (macOS 15 or later, per its App Store listing) as well as iPhone and iPad. According to the listing, one Pro subscription covers all three. It’s useful when you need to:

  • Clean up recordings: Noise Reduction, Noise Gate, Click and Echo Removal, Voice Enhance, Find Clipping and Clip Fix.
  • Remove or isolate vocals for karaoke or a cappella versions.
  • Change pitch, speed and tempo independently.
  • Export to many formats: 28 in total, including FLAC, ALAC, OGG, OPUS, M4B audiobooks and M4R ringtones, with control over bitrate, bit depth and sample rate.
  • Start on iPhone, finish on Mac, using the same app on both.

If all you need is to pull audio out of video files, MP3 Audio Converter also has a native Mac version (macOS 14 or later) and converts offline.

Choosing the right tool

Task Built-in option When to use a dedicated editor
Trim start and end QuickTime Player You also need fades or cuts in the middle
Convert to MP3 Music app You need FLAC, OGG, OPUS or exact bitrate settings
Multi-track mix GarageBand You also need noise reduction or vocal tools
Remove hiss or hum — Always: macOS has no built-in noise reduction
