How to Record a Reaction Video on iPhone

Three ways to record reaction videos on iPhone, from built-in screen recording with your mic to face-cam overlays, plus audio and copyright tips.

By AppLayer Studio · Last reviewed October 24, 2026 · Checked on iOS 17 and later

In this guide

  1. Method 1: Built-in screen recording with your voice
  2. Method 2: Face-cam reaction with Screen Recorder
  3. Method 3: React to the real world with two cameras
  4. Get your audio right
  5. Copyright and platform rules

Short answer: for a voice-only reaction, use iPhone’s built-in Screen Recording with the microphone turned on. To show your face, use an app that puts your front camera in a picture-in-picture bubble over the video, such as the Video Commentary tool in Screen Recorder: Video Edits. Either way, make sure you’re allowed to use the clip you’re reacting to.

Method 1: Built-in screen recording with your voice

  1. Add Screen Recording to Control Center if it isn’t there: open Control Center, tap + (or long-press an empty area) and add the Screen Recording control.
  2. Open Control Center and touch and hold the Screen Recording button.
  3. Tap Microphone to turn it on, then Start Recording.
  4. Play the video and talk over it.
  5. Tap the red indicator at the top of the screen to stop. The recording is saved to Photos.

This records your voice but not your face. Note that video from many streaming apps appears black in screen recordings because the content is DRM-protected. This is an iOS restriction that no app can bypass.

Method 2: Face-cam reaction with Screen Recorder

Screen Recorder: Video Edits includes creator tools built for this, according to its App Store listing:

  • Video Commentary: react to a video with your face cam in a circle, square or rectangle overlay.
  • Audio Commentary: record a voice-over on top of a video and balance the levels.
  • Multi-track editor: trim the source clip, add text and stickers, and change the aspect ratio for TikTok, Reels, Shorts or YouTube.
  • Screen recording in up to 4K at 60 fps with no watermark, for reacting to apps and games.

Screen recording itself is free in the app. Unlimited exports, 4K/1080p and all creator tools require Premium.

Method 3: React to the real world with two cameras

If you’re reacting to something in front of you, such as unboxing a product, tasting food or watching an event, record with both cameras at once. DualShot has a FaceCam picture-in-picture mode and can save vertical and horizontal versions from the same take. See how to record with front and back cameras.

Get your audio right

Reaction videos live or die on audio balance:

  • Use headphones while recording so the source audio isn’t picked up twice by your mic.
  • Lower the source volume so your voice sits clearly on top. Adjust the mix in the editor afterward.
  • Record in a quiet, soft room. See how to reduce background noise.

Copyright and platform rules

Reacting to someone else’s video doesn’t automatically make it fair use. Platforms may mute, block or remove reactions that use too much of the original. To reduce risk:

  • Add real commentary, analysis or criticism, not just a playthrough.
  • Use only as much of the original as you need.
  • Check the platform’s policies, and when in doubt, ask the creator for permission.

This isn’t legal advice. Rules vary by country and platform.
